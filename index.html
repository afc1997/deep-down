Deep
Down

By Fisherman & Trout

Logline

Amid the Scottish highlands, a drifter seeks shelter in a rotting village where a siren's call from the town's well lures him towards his darkest desires.

Synopsis

Deep Down is a dark fable following Quinn, a wounded American drifter on the run from past crimes, who finds himself in the remote Scottish village of Freuhman.

Looking for a chance of anonymity and enough cash to escape the isle, Quinn finds shelter on a rotting farm with Godfrey, a paranoid old recluse.

One night, he finds deep within the farmer's well, a Siren. Visions begin to plague him: dreams of this beautiful yet inhuman creature calling from the depths, tempting him toward her night after night.

As Quinn tries to escape the siren's grasp, he becomes entangled with the villagers, each carrying secrets and shame of their own.

As Quinn repeats his past mistakes, he succumbs to his temptation, infecting Freuhman, causing women across Freuhman, young and old, to become suddenly and violently pregnant. Their bodies are overtaken by something they cannot control.

The town soon realizes Quinn is to blame for this curse.

Theme

This isn't really a movie about a man who discovers a monster, it is a movie about how the monsters we carry will destroy us… if we let them.

With this film, we set out to explore the corrosive nature of shame. We wanted to examine how running from guilt only causes it to balloon inside us, trapping us in a self-fulfilling prophecy: I am bad because I have done bad, and therefore I will always be bad.

The entity in the well is a physical manifestation of Quinn's suppressed desires, his self-loathing, and his tendency to choose short-term comfort over real commitment. Surrounding him, the townspeople serve as a mirror, each reflecting a different facet of shame.

Ultimately, our goal is to anchor the audience inside Quinn's emotional reality. To tell a story where the internal becomes external. A journey from deep self-hatred and fear of the past toward a place of acceptance, vulnerability, and maturity.

Quinn

An American Navy vet on the run from his own guilt, landing in the damp isolation of Freumhan. He tries to bury his past through drinking, distance, and avoiding responsibility at all costs.

Just as he begins to form real connection and reach for redemption, the siren emerges. Tempting him to abandon his progress and repeat his past mistakes.

Godfrey

A frail 75-year-old farmer rotting alongside his fungus-tainted barley fields.

Bound to his crumbling land by a strange sense of obligation, he swings unpredictably between quiet hospitality and sudden, paranoid violence.

Fiona

A 25-year-old pub worker stranded in a dead-end village. Raised by her grandmother after being abandoned, she harbors a quiet desperation to escape.

She reaches out to Quinn as a link to the outside world, offering genuine connection only to be caught in the collateral damage of his psychological unraveling.

Colin

A boisterous war veteran and town leader who uses easy charm to mask a dangerous, controlling edge.

He steps into Quinn's life as a welcoming mentor. Offering work, drinks, and war stories—while hiding a violent, abusive side behind closed doors.

Siren

An ethereal threat waiting in the dark water of Godfrey's well.

Hypnotic and porcelain-skinned, she preys on Quinn's lust, using surreal visions and a distorted hum to slowly pull him out of reality and into the dark.

Camera

Our camera should feel haunting. Covered in mist, darkness, always presenting even the most impossible circumstances as objectively real.

We want the visuals to transmit the feeling that this town is rotting away. Thick grain, deep focus, incredibly textural.

Production Design

Scotland. Cobblestone farmhouses half-sunk into the earth. Hay-roofed buildings. A concrete factory chimney that screams.

Yellow barley heavy with black fungus. Hand-carved chairs without tables. Cracked glasses caked in dust. The silver pearlescent flask. The faded portrait of The Woman, black-and-white and old as the well.

Music

We want something abrasive and atonal, built from drones and dissonant strings that sit underneath every scene like something is wrong.

The music should feel like a combination between industrial and folkloric. Alluding to both the closed down factory and the mythical elements within the film.

Fisherman & Trout

Alex Fischman Cárdenas and Trout Cohen are a writer-director duo who met at a urinal on their first day at NYU. Since then, they've collaborated on narrative projects blending genre storytelling with surreal themes. Their work has earned Vimeo Staff Picks and screened at festivals such as Sundance, Clermont-Ferrand, Sitges, and AFI Fest.
