A film by Fisherman & Trout

Deep
Down

Logline

Amid the Scottish highlands, a drifter seeks shelter in a rotting village where a siren's call from the town's well drags him into obsession and terror.

Synopsis

Deep Down is a dark fable following Quinn, a wounded American drifter on the run from past crimes, who finds himself in the remote Scottish village of Freuhman. Looking for a chance of anonymity and enough cash to escape the isle, Quinn finds shelter on a rotting farm with Godfrey, a paranoid old recluse. As Quinn gets entangled with the townsfolk; something otherworldly stirs beneath the soil. Deep within the farmer's well, a Siren calls to him.

Visions begin to plague him: dreams of this beautiful yet inhuman creature calling from the depths, tempting him toward her night after night.

As Quinn tries to escape the siren's grasp, he becomes entangled with the villagers, each carrying secrets and shame of their own. But as Quinn repeats his past mistakes, he succumbs to his temptation, infecting the town, causing women across Freuhman, young and old, to become suddenly and violently pregnant. Their bodies overtaken by something they cannot control.

Theme

Deep Down presents a mystery about trauma, male repression, and the slow collapse of identity. For us, Quinn's journey is an allegory for the corrosive effects of shame, in particular, the shame of lust in men. Lust in this story is not romantic or freeing; it's haunting, spectral, and tied to Quinn's sense of self-loathing. The woman from the well is a manifestation of his forbidden thoughts, of the things he was told to repress as a soldier, as a man, as someone trying desperately to appear "normal" in a world that doesn't have space for him.

Quinn

Rugged, lean, eyes like a starving coyote. Vietnam veteran, now two years drifting Europe by train and foot. He arrives in Freumhan with a bleeding right hand, a green military pack, and no reason to stop — but something about this rotten corner of Scotland won't let him leave.

Quinn operates through charm and neediness in equal measure. He works hard, drinks harder, and looks at things he shouldn't. He's running from whatever happened on that Edinburgh street. The well is the first honest thing he's found.

Colin

Wide and tall. Dressed all in black. A dark beard hides his face. He enters rooms like a bear in search of honey — slowly, casually, but with total intention. He already knows Quinn is a soldier when they meet. He already knows about the military pack.

Colin is connected to the factory, connected to Fiona, connected to a web of knowledge he volunteers slowly, a piece at a time. He's the town's center of gravity and he knows it.

Fiona

Light brown hair, big hazel eyes. Unmistakably beautiful. She works the pub, hauls the deliveries alone down the road, lives two doors down from the bar her mother runs. She's never been anywhere else and she knows exactly what that means.

"I'm stuck knowing I'm Scottish." She says it laughing, but she means it. Quinn is the first person who's looked at Freumhan and called it beautiful. She doesn't know whether to trust that.

Siren

Porcelain white skin. Jet black hair, slicked by water. Big white eyes ringed in black. She stands naked at the bottom of Godfrey's stone well, holding the bucket in both hands, and looks up at Quinn.

She smiles. She disappears. Godfrey says the tainted land does this — poisons the mind, makes men see things. Her portrait, black and white and old, sits on Godfrey's bedside table. Quinn steals the flask from beside it.

Godfrey

Small, frail, wrinkles as deep as cuts. He plows his diseased fields alone. His silver pearlescent flask never leaves his pocket. He covers the well. He hasn't lived anywhere else since before the first war. "The big one."

"Got things here. Obligations of the earth that keep me BOUND." He can erupt without warning — slaps Quinn in the face, grabs him by the collar, then sets supper on the table thirty minutes later. The farm is dying. He won't leave. Something in the well is the reason.

Camera

Handheld but controlled — the camera lives close to Quinn, close enough to smell sweat and soil. Wide lenses on exteriors so the Scottish landscape swallows figures whole. Rack focus into fog; let backgrounds dissolve. Flashlight cinematography for night sequences. The well is always shot from above: one circular beam into black water.

Production Design

Scotland, 1971. Cobblestone farmhouses half-sunk into the earth. Hay-roofed buildings. A concrete factory chimney that screams. Yellow barley heavy with black fungus. Hand-carved chairs without tables. Cracked glasses caked in dust. The silver pearlescent flask. The faded portrait of The Woman, black-and-white and old as the well.

Music

Penderecki-esque strings — the script calls for them by name at the title card. Dissonant, ancient, not decorative. Sound as physical sensation. Scottish folk melody at its most corroded edge. Silence used as an instrument. The well has its own sound.

Sound Design

The factory screams in industrial agony. Leather boots on cobblestone. Water splashing in pitch black. The sheep's BAAAA cutting through the night. Sirens that overtake all sound. Wind rushing over the highlands. The soil is never fully quiet. Everything in Freumhan has a sound underneath it.

Edit

Slow. Patient. Let silences breathe until the audience holds their breath. The script's key editorial device: "ALL OF THE FOLLOWING HAPPENS WITHIN A MOMENT; A BRIEF GLANCE." The film moves at the pace of farm labor — then cuts like a knife. The well sequences are pure montage: darkness, a beam, a face.

Fisherman & Trout

Alex Fischman Cárdenas and Trout Cohen are a writer-director duo who met at a urinal on their first day at NYU. Their work has earned Vimeo Staff Picks and screened at Sundance, Clermont-Ferrand, Sitges, and AFI Fest.
